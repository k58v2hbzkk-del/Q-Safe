01 · The problem

Every wallet today has one lock.

It’s an elliptic-curve key. The day a large quantum computer runs Shor’s algorithm, anyone who has seen your public key can work out your private key, and your first payment shows it to the world.

02 · The switch

So we took that key out.

A QSafe account cannot be spent with an ECDSA signature at all. Not by you, not by a thief, not by someone who cracked your old wallet. Only a hash-based signature moves the money.

03 · How it signs

Sixty-seven chains, fifteen links each.

Winternitz signatures use nothing but keccak256. A quantum computer gets only a square-root speed-up against a hash, which still leaves 2128 work. Your phone signs in about 50 ms.

04 · One-time keys

A new key, every payment.

Each payment names the next key, and the contract rotates it on the spot. A used key is dead, so a replayed signature is worthless. Every key comes from one recovery code you keep.

05 · Q-Day, rehearsed

We attacked it. On-chain.

Our live demo sent a forged payment from the account’s ECDSA key and replayed an old signature. Aevum rejected both. Try it yourself: it costs nothing.
